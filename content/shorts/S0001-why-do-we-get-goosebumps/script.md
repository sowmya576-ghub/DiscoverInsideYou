### Clean read

Goosebumps are tiny muscles pulling your hairs upright — and you can't stop them. Your hair follicles are anchored to thin strands of smooth muscle called arrector pili. When you're cold, scared, or deeply moved, your sympathetic nervous system — your body's autopilot — signals them. They contract, tilting hairs up and lifting the skin into bumps. In furry animals, that raised coat traps air and holds in warmth. We have so little body hair that scientists think it barely warms us. But it may be more than a leftover: in a 2020 mouse study, the same nerve and muscle helped control hair-growing stem cells. So when music gives you goosebumps, that's an ancient warming reflex — still working inside you.
