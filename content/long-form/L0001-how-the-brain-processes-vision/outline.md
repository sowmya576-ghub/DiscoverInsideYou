## Central question

How does your brain turn light entering your eyes into the world you see?

## Editorial thesis

Seeing isn't a camera recording. Your eyes turn light into electrical signals, and your
brain builds the picture from them: sorting, routing, interpreting, and even filling in
what's missing.

## Why this matters to the viewer

Everything you're looking at right now, including this screen, is the brain's
reconstruction. Understanding how makes everyday experiences like the blind spot, optical
illusions, and night vision make sense.

## Opening (≈ 0:00–0:30)

Hook (accurate, specific): *"Right now, the image on the back of your eyes is upside
down. And your brain never turns it back over."* (C2, C14)

Then the question: so how do you see the world the right way up, in color, in depth,
instantly? Promise: follow a beam of light from this screen all the way to the back of
your brain. No greeting, no channel intro.

## Conclusion (≈ 0:30)

Return to the hook: the image is still upside down, and it doesn't matter, because seeing
happens in the brain, not the eye. The thesis lands: what you see is your brain's best
interpretation of the light it receives. Optional sign-off: "Discover Inside You — your
body, your world, uncovered."
