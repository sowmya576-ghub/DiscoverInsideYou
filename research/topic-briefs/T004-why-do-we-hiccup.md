## Audience question

What actually is a hiccup, and why does my body do it?

## Current scientific answer (preliminary)

A hiccup is a sudden, involuntary contraction of the diaphragm and other breathing-in
muscles, followed about 35 milliseconds later by the glottis snapping shut. That closure
makes the "hic." It's thought to be driven by a reflex circuit in the brainstem, and common
triggers include a stretched stomach. **Why** we hiccup is unknown. Leading ideas are an
evolutionary leftover from gill or tadpole breathing, or a reflex that helps suckling
infants clear swallowed air. Neither is proven.

## Hook ideas (must be accurate)

- "In every hiccup, your airway snaps shut about 35 milliseconds after a sudden gasp begins." (the 35 ms is the lag between muscle contraction and glottis closure; check exact phrasing against Howes 2012)
- The uncertainty itself: "Scientists know exactly how you hiccup. They still don't know why."
