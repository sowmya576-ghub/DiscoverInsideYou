## Audience question

Why do my fingers go wrinkly in the bath? Is it just water soaking in?

## Current scientific answer (preliminary)

Water-induced wrinkling is an active process controlled by the sympathetic nervous system:
blood vessels in the fingertip constrict, which changes the skin's shape. Fingers with
certain nerve damage, and replanted fingers, don't wrinkle. Whether wrinkles help grip is
debated. One study found faster handling of wet objects, a replication attempt found no
effect, and a large but less controlled study found wrinkles reduced the grip force needed
for wet objects.

## Hook ideas (must be accurate)

- "Wrinkly bath fingers aren't waterlogged skin — they're your nervous system at work."
- Myth correction: "It's not just water soaking in."

## Uncertainty and caveats

- Grip function is contested. The video must present both sides or frame it as an open question.
- Kareklas 2013 sample size not verified (Royal Society site returned 403).
- The exact trigger (sweat ducts, electrolytes) is proposed, not settled.
- Avoid any diagnostic suggestion about nerve damage.

## Misconceptions to address

- "Fingers wrinkle because skin absorbs water and swells." Nerves are required, so that's not the whole story.
